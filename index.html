Abstract

Large-scale egocentric human demonstrations provide a scalable source of embodied supervision for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, but effectively leveraging them for downstream VLA post-training remains challenging because human demonstrations vary in their relevance to a target robot and task distribution. We introduce ReWeight, a relevance-aware framework that selectively incorporReWeights egocentric human demonstrations into VLA post-training. EgoWeight learns a cross-embodiment visuomotor representation to retrieve relevant human demonstrations and assign sample-specific weights according to fine-grained human-robot discrepancy. Across eight RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and four real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, ReWeight consistently improves over robot-only post-training and naive human-robot data mixing.

Simulation Results: RoboTwin 2.0

RoboTwin 2.0 — VLA Post-Training: ReWeight selectively retrieves and weights egocentric human demonstrations for post-training, consistently outperforming both Robot-Only training and Mixed Data (Random) human-robot data mixing across eight simulation tasks. The results show that relevant and fine-grained weighted human experience is more effective than indiscriminately adding human demonstrations.
